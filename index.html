About Me

I am a third-year Ph.D. student in the Department of Statistics at North Carolina State University, advised by Professor Srijan Sengupta. My research focuses on developing statistically rigorous and computationally scalable methods for network data analysis. My recent work introduces predictive subsampling techniques for scalable inference in random graph models, enabling accurate statistical inference in massive networks while substantially reducing computational costs. More broadly, I am interested in causal inference, federated learning, model selection, and machine learning.

Prior to joining NC State, I earned my Master of Statistics and Bachelor of Statistics (Hons) from the Indian Statistical Institute and worked as a Data Scientist at Tata Digital, where I developed recommendation systems, forecasting models, customer analytics solutions, and experimentation frameworks.

I enjoy working on problems that bridge statistical theory and real-world applications and welcome opportunities for collaboration in network science, machine learning, and modern statistical methodology.

Research Projects

Dissertation  ·  Jan 2024 – Jun 2026

Predictive Subsampling for Scalable Inference in Large Networks

Proposed a subsample-based estimation algorithm for large, sparse RDPG networks achieving a 20× reduction in computational time with less than 50% increase in estimation error. Also developed a bootstrap hypothesis testing framework for robust inference in high-dimensional sparse networks.

Preprint → Code →

Ongoing  ·  Sep 2025 – Present

Monotone Constrained Link Prediction for Matched Networks

Developing a method to predict missing links in a partially observed network by borrowing information from a matched, fully observed network under monotonicity constraints rather than strict equality of underlying probability matrices.

In Progress

Ongoing  ·  Sep 2025 – Present

Model Selection via Predictive Subsampling in Networks

Developing a model selection framework for diverse network types using cross-validation and predictive subsampling to substantially reduce computational complexity, extending the subsampling paradigm to the model comparison problem.
